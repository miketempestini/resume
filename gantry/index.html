Why Gantry

Stop prompting. Start assigning.

Your agents already write the code. Gantry gives them a real plan to work from, and gives you a board that shows what each one is doing.

Private by design

Gantry runs entirely on your computer. Every project is a file on your own disk, and there's no server anywhere holding your roadmap.

  • no cloud
  • no login

Industry-standard planning

Epics, features, stories, bugs and subtasks. A backlog, a board, a roadmap, sprints and releases, ready the moment you open it.

Features

Everything a product team plans with.
Built for agents too.

01 / 04 · AI agents

Talk to your agents, right in the ticket

Mention @claude on any ticket and ask for help. The agent replies with a plan, waits for your yes, then claims the work and moves the ticket as it goes.

  • Agents claim tickets, so two never work on the same thing
  • Nothing changes until you reply yes
  • Every agent edit is logged, with Undo
  • Frontier or local, any MCP agent can read and update tickets

Live replies work with Claude today. You can mention any agent on a ticket. Claude answers on its own through its live channel (experimental). Others, like Codex, reply once you tell them in chat to check their mentions.

02 / 04 · Backlog and board

From epic to subtask, in one tree

Structure work as epics, features, user stories, bugs and subtasks. Do it by hand, or just ask your agent: through Gantry's built-in AI connection (MCP), it can create, edit, organize and schedule anything here for you.

Just ask

“Break the onboarding epic into stories, estimate them and put them in Sprint 3.”

  • Agents create, edit, move and schedule tickets for you
  • Inline editing for status, priority, sprint and dates
  • Filters, saved views and bulk edit
  • A Kanban board with your own statuses

03 / 04 · Roadmap and sprints

See the whole plan, let your agent shape it

A Gantt roadmap with a Today line, sprints, releases and milestones. Ask your agent to plan it: it reads every ticket, sets dates, flags what won't fit, and redraws the plan when priorities change. You approve the plan, and every change can be undone.

Just ask

“Plan Q1 so every feature ships by March 31. Put Apple Health before Widgets.”

  • An agent drafts the roadmap from your backlog
  • Reprioritize in a sentence: dates, sprints and dependencies follow
  • Warnings for late work, missed due dates and blocked starts
  • Drag to schedule, export as PNG or PDF

04 / 04 · Releases

Plan the release, ship it with notes

Plan each release with a target date and watch it fill as tickets finish. Say “ship it” and your agent shows you what goes out, then merges to production, files every ticket under the release and writes a short note for each from its own description, comments and commits. Read them over, then send a clean PDF.

Just ask

“Push release v3.0 to production and write the notes.”

  • Planned releases with target dates and live progress
  • Move unfinished tickets to the next release in one step
  • Notes come from the ticket, never made up
  • Highlights up top, exported as a clean PDF
